Understanding the results

w:1 asks the primary to acknowledge. w:majority asks a voting majority; this kit needs two data-bearing members. All three uses w:3. MongoDB does not provide a built-in w:"all" mode.

j:true waits for journal durability on the required members. j:false does not disable journaling. With MongoDB 7’s default majority journal setting, majority acknowledgments can still wait for journaled writes. Expect differences with hardware and load; there is no guaranteed latency ratio.

One disconnected secondary leaves a voting majority. With both secondaries disconnected, the isolated primary eventually steps down. Even w:1 then fails; write concern alone does not turn MongoDB into an always-available system. An acknowledgment timeout does not mean the write was undone. Timeline states are the observer’s view, sampled approximately once per second, not exact internal election timestamps.
